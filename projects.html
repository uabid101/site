6-DOF Rigid Multibody Dynamics Simulator

Context

Personal project built ahead of an end-of-studies internship in aerospace numerical simulation, to cover the technical chain expected in that kind of role: physical modelling, validated time integration, mechanical joints, and formal testing.

Objective

Simulate a rigid body with six degrees of freedom, with realistic joints that stay numerically stable, and compare two ways of modelling them (Lagrange multipliers and penalty method) instead of picking one by default.

Tasks

I wrote the Newton–Euler dynamics with an orientation quaternion and a fourth-order Runge–Kutta integrator, validated on its own against an exponential decay before use. Ball and pin joints use Lagrange multipliers with Baumgarte stabilization, the slider uses a penalty method, and an event sequencer can switch a joint on mid-simulation. Everything is checked by a pytest suite (physical invariants and non-regression snapshots).

Results

RK4 matches the analytical solution to within 10⁻⁶, Lagrange joints drift by less than 1% over 5 s, and energy is conserved to within 1% over 10 s of free asymmetric rotation. The main lesson is about limits: the slider joint is singular with Lagrange multipliers at perfect alignment, and activating a Lagrange constraint on a moving body can diverge, so those cases use the penalty method and the limits are documented.

GPU Chip Cooling via Microchannels

Context

A data-centre GPU like the H100 dissipates 700 W over about 800 mm², a heat flux of roughly 875 kW/m², far more than a classic finned heat sink can remove.

Objective

Design and validate numerically a liquid microchannel cooling system keeping the wall below 85 °C while the flow stays laminar (Re < 2000), which is the validity limit of the physical model.

Tasks

I validated a Poiseuille solver against the analytical solution, then wrote a 2D thermal CFD in Python (finite differences) and swept channel height, pressure drop and number of channels. The thermomechanical stress in the silicon/copper stack was computed with FEniCSx, and 79 validated CFD runs trained Random Forest and XGBoost surrogate models.

Results

A single channel was not enough, and shrinking it made overheating worse. The retained design uses 112 parallel 50 µm channels (Re = 571) and gives a wall temperature of 62.2 °C, with a simulated Nusselt number of 4.03 against 4.36 in theory (−7.6%) and an energy balance of 0.998. The FEM gives a maximum von Mises stress of 47 MPa in the silicon, a safety factor of 6.4, and XGBoost predicts wall temperature in under 1 ms with R² = 1.00 (on a regular parametric grid, so likely optimistic).
